We consider the unconstrained optimization problem
\begin{equation}
    \underset{x \in \bbR^{n}}{\mathrm{minimize}} \quad f(x),
    \label{prob:main_problem}
\end{equation}
where $f\colon \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is a continuously differentiable nonconvex function, and $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the optimization variable.
We focus on settings where objective function evaluations are contaminated by bounded and non-diminishing numerical noise~\citep{sunTrustRegionMethod2023}.
Specifically, only inexact function values $\overline{f}(x)$ are available for optimization.
Such inaccuracies are unavoidable in many realistic scenarios, including finite-precision arithmetic, simulation-based evaluations, and stochastic approximations~\citep{shiNoiseTolerantQuasiNewtonAlgorithm2022,moreEstimatingComputationalNoise2011,caflischMonteCarloQuasiMonte1998}.
When function values are contaminated by numerical noise, standard optimization algorithms may lose their theoretical guarantees, behave erratically, or terminate prematurely with unreliable solutions.

In the absence of noise and with accurate function evaluations, quasi-Newton methods, in particular the Broyden--Fletcher--Goldfarb--Shanno (BFGS) method and its \MPCchanged{limited-memory} variant (L-BFGS), are among the most efficient and widely used algorithms \MPCchanged{for solving problem~\eqref{prob:main_problem}~\citep{liuLimitedMemoryBFGS1989,nocedal1999numerical}}.
Their success is largely attributed to fast local convergence and scalability.
Standard implementations~\citep{2020SciPy-NMeth,Okazaki2007libLBFGS} rely on line search procedures that enforce the Wolfe conditions to guarantee sufficient descent and the positive definiteness of the Hessian approximation.

In the presence of numerical noise, these line search conditions may become unreliable.
Differences in function values or directional derivatives can be dominated by numerical errors, leading to unstable step sizes, ill-conditioned Hessian approximations, or abnormal termination~\citep{shiNoiseTolerantQuasiNewtonAlgorithm2022,paquetteStochasticLineSearch2020}.
From a computational perspective, improving the robustness of quasi-Newton methods in noisy environments is therefore crucial.
In particular, it is desirable to design algorithms that (i) retain computational efficiency when function evaluations are accurate, (ii) remain stable when function values are unreliable, and (iii) guarantee convergence to first-order stationary points under mild assumptions.

In this work, we propose a noise-tolerant regularized quasi-Newton method that addresses these challenges.
Our approach combines \MPCchanged{a relaxed Armijo} line \MPCchanged{search,} quadratic \MPCchanged{regularization \citep{uedaRegularizedNewtonMethod2014,kanzowRegularizationLimitedMemory2023},} and \MPCchanged{an OFFO-inspired} strategy~\citep{Gratton08022024,grattonOFFOMinimizationAlgorithms2023}.
\begin{MPCchange}
The closest regularized L-BFGS result is Theorem~1 of Kanzow and Steck~\citep{kanzowRegularizationLimitedMemory2023}.
Under bounded quasi-Newton matrices and a lower-bounded objective, it proves that the gradient norm has limit inferior zero, but assumes reliable function values and gives no iteration-complexity bound.
Our result instead gives the order-optimal \(\order{1/\varepsilon^2}\) bound for smooth nonconvex optimization under persistent bounded relative-or-absolute function errors~\citep{carmonLowerBoundsFinding2020}.
Rather than merely relaxing the line search, our method uses noisy function values only while they certify a reference decrease and otherwise switches to an objective-function-free regularization scale.
It thereby retains useful line-search behavior without making continued progress depend on unreliable comparisons.

OFFO methods avoid unreliable function values altogether~\citep{Gratton08022024,grattonOFFOMinimizationAlgorithms2023}, whereas our method uses them selectively.
The noise-tolerant quasi-Newton analysis of Shi et al.~\citep{shiNoiseTolerantQuasiNewtonAlgorithm2022} permits both function and gradient errors but assumes strong convexity and converges linearly only to a noise-dependent neighborhood.
Its stationarity radius contains a function-error contribution proportional to \(\sqrt{M\epsilon_f}\) and a gradient-error contribution proportional to \(\epsilon_g\), so keeping the former below the latter requires \(\epsilon_f=\order{\epsilon_g^2/M}\) up to problem-dependent constants.
In particular, even when \(\epsilon_g=0\), their bound retains a threshold proportional to \(\sqrt{M\epsilon_f}\), whereas a fixed \(0\leq\epsilon_f<1\) changes the constants in our bound but imposes no stationarity floor.
This distinction follows from our adaptively recomputed error allowance and the $K^0/K^+$ switch, which stops relying on function comparisons when they cease to certify progress.
Shi et al. cover bounded gradient errors and protect curvature pairs by lengthening, whereas our arbitrary-accuracy and complexity guarantees require exact gradients.
\end{MPCchange}

\begin{MPCchange}
The rates in \cref{tab:related_work_comparison} have different targets and assumptions.
For line-search L-BFGS with exact evaluations, Theorem~7.1 of Liu and Nocedal~\citep{liuLimitedMemoryBFGS1989} proves R-linear convergence of the objective gap under uniform convexity and bounded initial scaling matrices.
For NTQN, Theorem~3.5 of Shi et al.~\citep{shiNoiseTolerantQuasiNewtonAlgorithm2022} proves \(\phi(x_k)-\phi^*\leq\rho^k(\phi(x_0)-\phi^*)+2\epsilon_f\), where \(0<\rho<1\), until the iterates enter a noise-dependent neighborhood.
Thus NTQN has an order-level linear rate under strong convexity, but does not guarantee an arbitrary smaller stationarity tolerance.
By contrast, OFFO and our method have \(\order{1/\varepsilon^2}\) worst-case complexity for first-order stationarity on smooth nonconvex objectives, and our result additionally permits persistent bounded function errors.

When the observed function value certifies sufficient decrease, the method uses an unregularized quasi-Newton step.
Otherwise, an AdaGrad-Norm-type accumulator controls the regularization over iterations in $K^+$.
Under exact gradients and bounded relative-or-absolute function errors, the method reaches an \(\varepsilon\)-first-order stationary point in the order-optimal \(\order{1/\varepsilon^2}\) iterations.
The CUTEst study reports problem dimensions, stopping rules, sensitivity to the supplied error estimate, and ablations of the hybrid mechanism.
It separates theory-aligned function-noise experiments from joint function-and-gradient perturbations, which are deliberately adverse stress tests rather than evidence for the theorem.
\end{MPCchange}
