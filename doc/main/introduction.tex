We consider the unconstrained optimization problem
\begin{equation}
    \underset{x \in \bbR^{n}}{\mathrm{minimize}} \quad f(x),
    \label{prob:main_problem}
\end{equation}
where $f\colon \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is a continuously differentiable nonconvex function, and $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the optimization variable.
We focus on settings where objective function evaluations, especially the function values, are contaminated by bounded and non-diminishing numerical noise~\citep{sunTrustRegionMethod2023}.
Specifically, only inexact function values $\overline{f}(x)$ are available for optimization.
Such inaccuracies are unavoidable in many realistic scenarios, including finite-precision arithmetic, simulation-based evaluations, and stochastic approximations~\citep{shiNoiseTolerantQuasiNewtonAlgorithm2022,moreEstimatingComputationalNoise2011,caflischMonteCarloQuasiMonte1998}.

In the absence of noise, \MPCchanged{numerous optimization methods such as quasi-Newton and regularized quasi-Newton methods can be used to solve the problem~\eqref{prob:main_problem}.
For their success, the accuracy of function values is crucial.
For example, line-search methods use function values in sufficient-decrease tests~\citep{2020SciPy-NMeth,Okazaki2007libLBFGS}.
Adaptive regularization methods use the actual objective reduction to accept steps and adjust regularization~\citep{uedaConvergencePropertiesRegularized2010,uedaRegularizedNewtonMethod2014,kanzowRegularizationLimitedMemory2023}.}

In the presence of noise, these comparisons become unreliable, and the algorithms may lose their theoretical guarantees, behave erratically, or terminate prematurely with unreliable solutions~\citep{shiNoiseTolerantQuasiNewtonAlgorithm2022,paquetteStochasticLineSearch2020}.
\MPCchanged{There are two approaches that can address function-value errors.
The first is to use a noise-tolerant methods that account for bounded errors~\citep{shiNoiseTolerantQuasiNewtonAlgorithm2022}, such as relaxed Armijo tests~\citep{shiNoiseTolerantQuasiNewtonAlgorithm2022,paquetteStochasticLineSearch2020}.
The second is to avoid function values entirely, as in Objective-Function-Free Optimization (OFFO) methods~\citep{Gratton08022024,grattonOFFOMinimizationAlgorithms2023}.
However, as we will detail in \cref{sec:related_work}, these approaches have limitations.
Especially, disregarding reliable function values can be unnecessarily conservative in practice.}

These observations naturally motivate us to design noise-tolerant quasi-Newton methods that utilize function values when they are reliable and avoid them when they are not, while maintaining convergence guarantees.
\MPCchanged{Natural idea is to combine the former with the latter, switching between them.
The key difficulty is how to determine when to switch between the two methods.
If we overlook the noise, we may accept unreliable steps and lose convergence guarantees, being unstable or even diverging.
If we are too conservative, we may reject reliable steps and lose practical efficiency, slowing down the practical convergence speed.
Achieving a balance between these two extremes is crucial for designing effective noise-tolerant quasi-Newton methods.}

In this work, we propose a noise-tolerant regularized quasi-Newton method.
Our approach combines Armijo-type line search with quadratic regularization~\citep{uedaRegularizedNewtonMethod2014,kanzowRegularizationLimitedMemory2023}
and Objective-Function-Free Optimization (OFFO) inspired strategy~\citep{Gratton08022024,grattonOFFOMinimizationAlgorithms2023}.
When a sufficient decrease in function value is observed, the method behaves similarly to standard quasi-Newton methods with line search, preserving its practical efficiency.
When numerical noise dominates, we introduce regularization on the approximate Hessian to ensure numerical stability, which yields convergence properties similar to those of OFFO methods, particularly AdaGrad-Norm~\citep{wardAdaGradStepsizesSharp2020}.

\MPCchanged{The key methodological advance is the carefully designed, error-model-based switch between unregularized quasi-Newton steps and OFFO-inspired regularized steps.
A relaxed Armijo condition alone may accept steps without sufficient decrease in the true objective, so an additional condition is needed to preserve the convergence guarantee.
Our switch allows another unregularized step only after the observed objective decrease compensates for the relaxation used at the preceding unregularized iteration.
This leads to a flexible and adaptive switching strategy that can exploit reliable function while maintaining convergence guarantees under persistent function-value noise.}

We also conducted extensive numerical experiments on the CUTEst benchmark collection.
We examined function-value noise, joint function-and-gradient noise, error-bound misspecification, and reduced precision settings.
The proposed variants attain higher solved percentages with fewer oracle calls than the existing quasi-Newton baselines for almost all settings.
We also measured the per-iteration timing and elapsed time of the methods.
The per-iteration timing experiment shows our lower costs than the regularized L-BFGS baselines.
In the elapsed-time comparison, the proposed variants also shows superior performance to the existing quasi-Newton baselines, verifying that the proposed method is not only theoretically sound but also practically efficient in noisy environments.

\begin{MPCchange}
We next compare our method with related quasi-Newton and OFFO methods, whose theoretical guarantees are summarized in \cref{tab:related_work_comparison}.


The Broyden--Fletcher--Goldfarb--Shanno (BFGS) method and its limited-memory variant (L-BFGS) are standard quasi-Newton methods, with L-BFGS designed for large-scale optimization~\citep{liuLimitedMemoryBFGS1989,nocedal1999numerical}.

The first three methods in the table use function values, but their convergence theories do not allow function-value errors.

The closest methods differ from ours in how they use function values and handle evaluation errors.
The regularized L-BFGS method assumes reliable function values and establishes global convergence to first-order stationarity~\citep[Theorem~2]{kanzowRegularizationLimitedMemory2023}.

NTQN uses a noise-tolerant line search and a lengthening procedure to stabilize curvature information under bounded errors in function and gradient evaluations.
The NTQN analysis allows bounded gradient errors and establishes convergence to a noise-dependent neighborhood for strongly convex objectives~\citep[Theorem~3.5]{shiNoiseTolerantQuasiNewtonAlgorithm2022}.
Our relaxed Armijo test is related to their noise-tolerant test~\citep[Section~4]{shiNoiseTolerantQuasiNewtonAlgorithm2022}, whereas our switch bounds the sum of error allowances over unregularized iterations and yields an \(\order{1/\varepsilon^2}\) bound for arbitrarily small first-order tolerances on smooth nonconvex objectives with accurate gradients.

Regularized Newton and OFFO methods provide further theoretical context~\citep{uedaConvergencePropertiesRegularized2010,Gratton08022024,grattonOFFOMinimizationAlgorithms2023}.
Regularized Newton methods attain an \(\order{1/\varepsilon^2}\) iteration bound with reliable function values~\citep[Theorem~4.1]{uedaConvergencePropertiesRegularized2010}, while OFFO methods avoid function values and attain the same order for smooth nonconvex objectives~\citep{Gratton08022024,grattonOFFOMinimizationAlgorithms2023}.
Our method instead uses inexact function values to permit unregularized quasi-Newton steps when progress is certified and retains this worst-case order under persistent function-value errors.
\end{MPCchange}
